Episode 14: what a reader owes the page

A podcast episode carries its audio the way most of them do: nothing is in the markup the server sent. The player is assembled after load, the file hangs off a source child rather than a src attribute, and the episode metadata arrives as JSON-LD written by the same script. Every assertion on this page fails against the initial HTML.

The transcript below is the reason the page is still an article: the audio is the subject, but the prose is what dominates, and shape classification is decided by proportion rather than by the presence of a media element.

We talk about the difference between fetching a page and reading one, why a reader that runs scripts is not the same thing as a browser, and what it means to hand a medium back to the user instead of rendering it. The conversation runs to a little over half an hour and covers the settle policy, the extraction contract, and the long argument about whether a reader should ever play anything at all.
