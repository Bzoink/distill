A careful reader

The useful page is the one that leaves a reader with a clear understanding of what happened, why it matters, and what can be done next.

Extraction is not a request to flatten the web into a bag of words. It is a request to preserve the structure that carries meaning while removing the navigation, promotion, and chrome that surround it. Headings establish the shape of an argument, paragraphs carry its turns, and links keep the useful references attached to the sentence that introduced them.

A small reader can therefore be ambitious about content while remaining deliberately modest about presentation. It runs the page's own scripts until the content settles, then chooses a stable representation of the result. The source layout is evidence about structure, not a command to reproduce every pixel or interaction.

That boundary makes the result easier to test. A saved page can be run repeatedly, its title and text can be compared to a golden output, and a change in the extractor can be explained instead of mistaken for a redesign. The output should be calm, legible, and honest about what it could not keep.
