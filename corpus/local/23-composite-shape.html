The kingfisher's hunting technique

PENDING

The common kingfisher hunts from a perch above slow-moving water, holding position for long stretches before dropping. The dive itself lasts under a second, and the bird corrects its aim during descent to compensate for the refraction that displaces its target's apparent position.

A nictitating membrane closes over the eye at the moment of entry, so the final approach is made blind. The strike is committed before the bird can see the outcome, which is why the perch time matters more than the dive: the aim is fixed above the surface and cannot be revised below it.

Juveniles fail often enough that the first weeks after fledging carry high mortality. A bird that cannot reliably judge the refraction correction will not take enough fish to sustain the dive rate the technique demands, and the margin between a competent adult and an incompetent juvenile is measured in a handful of successful strikes per day.
